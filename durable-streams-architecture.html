offset = line number. A file you tail -f and a live socket are the same log — only durability & liveness differ. "Catch me up from X→Y" is just "read lines X→Y".

What you do / don't touch

● Adopt: log, offsets, resume, State→collections.

● Contribute: a pluggable transport seam in the client reader.

● Build: the hibernating-WS DO transport + server, and your createTransportDB facade.

Honest tradeoff

A WS tail gives up CDN-cacheability & request-collapsing — each socket is a stateful upstream. So pitch it as a Cloudflare deployment transport, not a protocol-core change. You're not fighting their thesis; you're adding the one transport that wins where statelessness doesn't.

Decisive unknown

How factored is the client reader today? That single fact = upstream PR vs thin fork. Next step: read @durable-streams/state + client source, locate the seam.

scenetest is just one consumer (①). Everything ②–⑦ is a reusable server-to-collection primitive: tell the server what to replicate & how to publish; the client figures out the transport, materializes N collections, hands back a map.
