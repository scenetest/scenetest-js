A → B is the whole point. Same application facts, source to screen. scene:start + scene:end are two appended facts; the scene row is a projection over them — they never share a row on the wire. Everything between A and B is generic, append-only plumbing we're abstracting away.

Where do verbs live? → one box

Only inside TransportDB's projection, client-side, derived (key exists ? update : insert). The wire, the sinks, the DO, the log are all append-only facts. This is option (c): facts up, verbs once at the bottom.

Is it a CRDT? → no

The DO is a single-threaded serializer = one total order; clients are read-only deterministic projections of it. No concurrent multi-writer merge ⇒ no CRDT. (That's what y-durable-streams is for; you don't need it.)

The simplification: stop putting verbs on the up-leg — there is no “verb encode” step. Producers append facts; the DO is a generic append-log; the projection at B derives verbs once. That makes the entire transport domain-blind — the reusable primitive from message #1 — and keeps your partial events (start/end) and the Waterfall timeline intact. Not (a) row-ops everywhere, not (b) DO-materializes — (c): append facts, project at the edge.
